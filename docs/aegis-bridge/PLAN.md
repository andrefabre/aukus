# PLAN.md — Project Aegis-Bridge: Go-to-Market Strategy

## Overview

A four-phase plan to build a profitable, defensible compliance advisory
practice serving AUKUS Pillar II SMEs in Western Australia. Growth is driven
by genuine expertise and demonstrated value, not manufactured urgency.

Target market: WA-based firms with 5–50 staff that have recently entered or
are actively pursuing AUKUS-related contracts and face real DISP / Essential
Eight obligations they lack internal capability to address.

Revenue target (12 months): 6 retainer clients at $3,500/month = $21,000 MRR.

---

## Phase 1 — Preparation: Build the Knowledge Asset (Weeks 1–4)

### Goal
Complete the Obsidian vault, intake tooling, and a pilot gap-analysis report
before approaching any prospective clients. Do not contact firms until the
product can actually deliver what it promises.

### Actions
1. Populate the `frameworks/` vault with ISM controls (June 2025 revision),
   Essential Eight Maturity Model, NIST 800-171 rev 2, and DISP requirements.
2. Build and test the cross-reference mappings (E8 ↔ ISM ↔ NIST).
3. Write the intake questionnaire in Tally. Test the CSV-to-vault import script.
4. Conduct a self-audit of a volunteer firm (friend, former colleague, or
   your own hypothetical business) to validate the gap-analysis template
   produces a genuinely useful output.
5. Write one worked example report (anonymised) that can be shown to prospects.

### Success Criteria
- Vault has complete coverage of E8 ML1–ML3 and ISM controls flagged as
  DISP-relevant.
- Gap analysis template produces a readable, accurate report in under 2 hours
  of analyst time.
- Intake form tested end-to-end.

---

## Phase 2 — Market Entry: Visibility & Inbound (Weeks 4–12)

### Goal
Build credibility in the WA defence SME community through genuine content and
targeted participation in existing channels, generating inbound enquiries.

### Actions

#### Content Marketing (low-cost, high-credibility)
- Publish a free "Essential Eight for Defence SMEs" plain-English guide on a
  simple website (Carrd, $19/yr) or GitHub Pages ($0). This is genuinely
  useful to the target market and establishes expertise.
- Write 3–4 LinkedIn articles explaining common DISP stumbling blocks for
  small firms. No product pitch — just useful information.
- Offer a free 30-minute "DISP readiness sanity check" call, publicly
  advertised. This builds pipeline and market understanding simultaneously.

#### Community Presence
- Join and participate in Henderson Alliance events and online forums as a
  genuine contributor, not as a sales activity.
- Engage with the WA AUKUS SME community on LinkedIn by commenting usefully on
  industry discussions.

#### Outbound (honest and targeted)
Identify firms from public government grant announcements (AUKUS SME Readiness
Fund recipients are published by name). These firms have a publicly confirmed
need for compliance uplift — the grant often requires it.

Outreach approach:
- Send a short, honest email or LinkedIn message: "I saw your firm received
  [grant]. I work with WA SMEs on DISP and Essential Eight readiness. If
  the compliance side is something you're working through, happy to have a
  no-obligation conversation."
- No fake reports. No manufactured urgency. A genuine offer of a conversation.

### What Not to Do
- Do not send unsolicited "reports" about firms' alleged security gaps derived
  from scraping their public websites. This is misleading and would likely
  breach ACL s18 (misleading conduct) if it implies a depth of analysis that
  wasn't performed.
- Do not imply government endorsement or affiliation.

## Phase 3 — Conversion: The Discovery Process (Ongoing from Week 8)

### Goal
Convert qualified conversations into paying engagements through an honest,
structured discovery and scoping process.

### The Discovery Call (60 min)
The purpose is to understand whether the firm has a genuine compliance
obligation and whether Aegis-Bridge can genuinely help. Not every firm will be
a fit, and saying so honestly builds long-term reputation.

Structure:
1. Understand their current DISP status and upcoming tender obligations (15 min)
2. Walk through the intake questionnaire categories at a high level to identify
   the most acute gaps (20 min)
3. Explain what a full gap analysis produces and what it won't tell them (10 min)
4. Discuss fit and whether to proceed (15 min)

### Engagement Options

| Product | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Gap Analysis Report | Full intake + cross-framework gap analysis + prioritised roadmap | $2,500 fixed |
| Remediation Advisory | 3-month engagement to implement the roadmap alongside their team | $4,500 fixed |
| Fractional Compliance Architect | Ongoing retainer: quarterly re-assessment, tender support, ISM update tracking | $3,500/month |

Pricing is based on real time investment and market rates for specialist
compliance work in the defence sector. It is not inflated by misleading
comparisons or fabricated urgency.

### Proposal
The proposal document clearly states:
- What is included and what is not
- How many hours of analyst time are allocated
- What the client is responsible for delivering
- The specific frameworks and controls covered

---

## Phase 4 — Retention: The Fractional Architect Model (Month 3+)

### Goal
Convert gap analysis clients into ongoing retainers, justified by genuine
recurring value: the compliance landscape changes, tenders require
re-attestation, and the ISM is updated regularly.

### What the Retainer Delivers (per month)
- Vault updated for any ISM or E8 changes affecting their control set
- One progress review call (30 min) to track remediation against the roadmap
- Support for any tender-specific compliance documentation requests
- Ad-hoc email Q&A (up to 2 hours analyst time)
- Updated compliance status view in their client portal

### Value Anchor (honest)
The genuine value proposition: a small WA defence firm does not employ a
full-time compliance architect. A fractional engagement provides that expertise
at a fraction of the cost of a hire, and keeps their DISP posture maintained
between tender cycles — which is a real operational requirement.

This is stated plainly, without inflated "saved cost" comparisons.
